# 0197 — The record sees the legs of a combination bought off the desk

**Status:** Accepted on Joe's approval of the 2026-11-11 plan. Numbered 0197 on `main`: 0196 was the highest.
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Schema:** v69 — two new tables (`venue_combo_legs`, `venue_combo_leg_refusals`) and two nullable columns (`pick_sources.tailed_from`, `parlay_lookups.tailed_from`; see ADR 0193 Amendment 1 for those).
**Tickets:** story #419 under #320; #420 (this ADR, the schema, the reader, the backfill), #421 (the live run), #422, #423, #424, #425.
**Extends:** ADR 0194 (the record reaches each leg of a combination Joe *holds*), ADR 0193 (where a pick came from), ADR 0016 (candlesticks are free and perishable).
**Leaves standing:** ADR 0063 (`gate.py` reads none of this), ADR 0071 §2.5 (a per-row fact is shown, never ranked by), ADR 0196 §2.1 (no threshold-triggered word at the buy moment), #320's refusal list.

## 1. What was missing

Joe tails parlays. He copies a combination that a sharp on Kalshi's own feed or a friend built, and he buys it in the Kalshi app (his answers by option button, 2026-11-11). On that day he asked what made the winning ones win, and whether the desk could carry the lesson.

The record could not say what any of them was made of. A combination bought in the Kalshi app reaches this instance as a `venue_settlements` row and nothing else: no `parlay_positions` row and no `parlay_lookups` row, which are the only two places `bets._legs_by_ticker` read. Most of his settled combinations were in that state. ADR 0194's per-leg record covers the legs he holds *through the desk*, so `by_leg_kind` was silent about them too.

What the record could say, from price alone, was read on a private page and is not repeated here (operator data stays out of the repo). In one sentence that carries no number: the off-desk tickets won about as often as their prices said, so nothing in the price-level record separates a winner from a loser except its price. Whether any *kind of leg* or any *named source* does differently is a question that needs the legs and the names, which is what this ADR and Amendment 1 to ADR 0193 build.

## 2. The decision

1. **The venue's own market is the authority on what a combination is made of, and it is asked once.** `mve_selected_legs` (`event_ticker`, `market_ticker`, `side` per leg) is still served after settlement, unauthenticated: `GET /markets/{ticker}`, and `GET /historical/markets/{ticker}` for the older tickers the first answers 404 for. Probed 2026-11-11 on three of his settled tickets, the oldest from August; `tests/fixtures/combo_settled_market_with_legs.json` is a stranger's settled combination off the public list, captured for the shape.
2. **All of a combination's legs or none.** One unreadable leg refuses the combination; `venue_combo_leg_refusals` keeps the reason, so a bounded pass does not re-read a permanent refusal every cycle and the count of each reason can be reported.
3. **A label is words, from the venue.** Where discovery has the leg's market, the label is its title, the convention `hedge.adopt_venue_combo` uses. Where it has not, the words come from the combination's own title, which lists one `yes <words>` or `no <words>` part per leg in leg order. Each part's prefix must equal its leg's side and the part count must equal the leg count, or the title is not used. Many of these legs are in sports the desk does not follow, so this second source is the common one.
4. **A leg's result is its market's own, and only once the market is `finalized`.** `kalshi_markets.result` where discovery resolved the market, else `venue_combo_legs.venue_result`, read once from the leg's market through discovery's own `read_market_result`. A `determined` result can still be reversed and this table never overwrites one, so it waits (`kalshi-platform`'s review of #420). Unread is NULL, shown as unread, and never counted as lost. A leg the venue had no final result for is stamped `result_read_ms` and left alone for six hours, so legs that cannot resolve yet never block the ones that can.
5. **`league` is the desk's own key where a held leg of the same series carries one, else the bare series ticker.** The series is the venue's grouping, so it is a fact, and it keeps one unfollowed sport's legs together instead of filing them all under "Unknown".
6. **Leg prices are filled like ADR 0194's, under one extra rule.** The candle ask at purchase and the close are read by `scripts/backfill_venue_combo_legs.py` with `backfill_leg_prices.py`'s own helpers. The purchase instant is `venue_settlements.position_first_seen_ms` only where `position_time_source = 'fill_created_time'`; a poll instant or a settlement time is not when he bought, and such a leg keeps a NULL ask. `price_read_ms` stamps a leg the pass has finished with, so a bounded run reaches the rest. **Bars are read from the live endpoint, then from the venue's archive:** `/series/{s}/markets/{t}/candlesticks` answered 404 on 2026-11-11 for a market that closed 2026-09-04 (68 days earlier, so inside ADR 0016's 80), and `/historical/markets/{t}/candlesticks` answered 200 for the same market and window. The archive's bar spells the close `close` where the live one spells it `close_dollars` (`tests/fixtures/candlesticks_historical_archive.json`); `archive_bar` renames that one key.
7. **Two writers, one module.** `backend/venue_combo_legs.py` holds the rules. The portfolio poller calls it on the five-minute cadence with a small per-pass ceiling (`VENUE_LEG_COMBOS_PER_PASS`, `VENUE_LEG_RESULTS_PER_PASS`), after everything the cycle exists for, committing per combination, never raising, and under a 45-second budget for the whole pass so a degraded venue cannot push the next balance read back. The script runs the same passes in bulk plus the price pass, through `instrument.yml` behind the typed-app-name guard, and prints counts only.
8. **No leg is counted twice.** A combination with a `parlay_positions` row is never written here, and `by_leg_kind_summary` reads this table only for combinations with no such row.
9. **Readers.** `_legs_by_ticker` takes this table as its third source, after the desk's two. `by_leg_kind_summary` unions it under ADR 0194 §2.6's rules unchanged: counts, fixed order, `too_few` below 5 expected each side, no ranking.

## 3. What this does not establish

- **Who built a combination, or that it was tailed at all.** A ticket bought off the desk may be his own pick. The name is his word (ADR 0193 Amendment 1), never inferred from this table.
- **That any sharp, sport or kind of leg beats its price.** This builds the columns. A claim needs its own registration, and CLAUDE.md's measurement rules apply: n before the effect, the parts before the pool, the price actually paid, and a count of the tests run.
- **How far back the archive's bars go.** ADR 0016 measured about 80 days on the live candlestick endpoint and that still holds for that endpoint. One August market answered from the archive on 2026-11-11; that is one market, not a retention figure. ADR 0194 §2.4 says the window on the held legs "closes around 2026-11-06, after which the remaining NULLs are permanent"; `scripts/backfill_leg_prices.py` never asked the archive, so that sentence is true of what the script reads and unproven of what the venue keeps. Re-running that backfill through the archive is its own ticket, not this ADR.
- **An ask for a leg whose quote had not moved in the fifteen minutes before he bought.** The price pass keeps ADR 0194's look-back (`WINDOW_MINUTES = 15`), and a bar exists only for a minute in which the quote changed. In a rehearsal on a scratch database (2026-11-11, three probed tickets, purchase times approximated from the market's creation time, so a count and not a rate), four August legs had archive bars sixteen to forty minutes before the approximated purchase and none inside the window; they stayed NULL. Widening the window trades coverage for a staler price and would make this pass's ask a different quantity from ADR 0194's, so it is not done here.
- **That a signed read of `/historical/markets/...` behaves like the unsigned one probed.** The poller signs; the probe did not. The first deployed pass's counts settle it.
- **A close for most of these legs.** The close needs an odds fixture linked to the leg's event, and the feed buys a handful of sports. Expect `no_link` to be the largest count in the price pass.
- **That the combination title's order equals the leg order when every leg has the same side.** The side check catches a reorder only where sides differ.
- **That a result read from `/historical/markets` means the same as one read at finalisation.** It is the same field on the same market; no case where the two disagree has been looked for.

## 4. Refused, and where

Ranking sharps or any "best source" line (ADR 0071 §2.5, ADR 0193). A price-band record or any band word on Check a parlay (ADR 0196 §2.1; the partner agent cut it from this plan on 2026-11-11 before it was built). Bulk-tagging past tickets as tailed (Joe: legs only, names going forward). Reading other traders' positions off Kalshi's social feed: no API for it is established, and scraping the app is out.
