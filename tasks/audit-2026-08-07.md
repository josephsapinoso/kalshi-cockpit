Four independent review agents audited the money path, the measurement
discipline, the Kalshi API layer, and the test suite for false confidence.
This file is the **complete triage**: what was fixed, what is outstanding, and
enough detail on each open item to act on it without re-deriving the finding.

Ordering within each section is by severity. Nothing here is closed by having
been written down.

## Fixed this session

### Money path

| # | Fix |
|---|---|
| M1 | **`/api/orders` ignored `suggested_contracts`.** `suppressed_reason IS NULL` does *not* mean bettable — the engine's "no edge" state is `suggested_contracts = 0` with no suppression reason. On the seeded demo, rows scored at −6.0c, −3.5c and −1.2c per contract were fully orderable at max size. The endpoint now refuses an unauthorised row and caps size at `min(requested, authorised, max_order_contracts)`. |

| M4 | **`calculate_fee` returned `0.0` for an untradeable price.** A zero fee on a zero-cost ask yields `effective_price` $0.00, `breakeven_win_rate` 0%, and `edge_after_fees_tenths` **+55c on a coin flip**. Now returns `None`; `effective_price`, `evaluate` and `kalshi_equivalent` refuse rather than compute. |
| M6 | **Freshness had no lower bound.** A `created_ms` written by a clock an hour ahead produced a *negative* age that passed a 30-second limit — the gate failed open, and harder the further the clock was wrong. Now refused. |
| M10 | `worst_case_cost_dollars` computed the fee at the rounded price. Now takes the max of both, since a field named `worst_case` must not round a cost down. |

## Open — ranked

### SEV 1: can open the money gate on noise, or produce wrong numbers

1. **Gate `n` counts non-independent observations.** `persist_recommendation` does an unconditional INSERT with no uniqueness on `(ticker, side)`, and every row for one ticker is scored against **one** closing line. With `k` recommendations per market the standard error is understated by ~`sqrt(k)`, so the 300-observation floor is reachable from ~10 markets polled 30 times. Both sides of a market are counted separately too. *Fix: cluster by ticker (or by game) before computing the standard error, and count distinct markets toward the floor.*

2. **Continuous monitoring with no correction (peeking).** `evaluate_gate` re-runs on every request against a growing database, with no pre-registered `n`, no alpha spending, no always-valid bound. Under a true zero-CLV process the probability the running z ever crosses 2 tends to 1. This is the multiple-comparisons lesson applied along the *time* axis, on the arming path for real money. *Fix: an always-valid confidence sequence, or a pre-registered evaluation cadence.*

3. **`backtest.beats_close` is a bare boolean contradicting its own verdict.** `disagreement_accuracy > market_accuracy` with no noise guard, sitting in the same dataclass as a `_verdict` that correctly says "inside the noise band, no demonstrated edge". Verbatim the 2026-08-07 lesson, in Python instead of SQL. The test asserts the verdict text but not the boolean, and its fixture makes both accuracies identical by luck.

4. **`margins.fit()` overwrites the published `sd` even on a thin sample.** The non-empirical branch then uses that corrupted `sd` for its normal approximation — the guard routes around thin data into a fallback also built from thin data. With `n = 1`, `max(1, 0)` gives variance 0 → **`sd = 0`** → `_normal_survival` returns exactly 1.0 or 0.0. One observation yields a certainty, which in Kelly sizing is an unbounded bet. `fit_by_spread` calls `fit` on every bucket including 1–3 game ones.

5. **`orders.py` deci-cent asks cannot fill.** `api_price_cents` floors to whole cents, so a 50.5c ask rests at 50c on exactly the ~25% of markets the deci-cent representation exists to serve. Safe for money, corrupting for the record: the paper log accumulates "placed" orders with no fills. *Needs verification against the live `POST /portfolio/orders` schema — currently an unrecorded assumption in the one module that can lose money.*

### SEV 2: correct statistic beside a contradicting presentation

6. **The Calibration panel renders both operands of a suppressed gap.** `implied_probability` and `actual_rate` are shown on every row; only the derived `gap_display` says `(noise)`. A reader subtracts two adjacent numbers and recovers the 20.8-point "finding" the guard exists to withhold. The page docstring claims otherwise. Same shape for `beat_close_rate` on rows reading "insufficient sample".

7. **`mart_multiple_comparisons` counts only `mart_calibration`'s tests.** `mart_clv_by_bucket` and `mart_suppression_audit` run their own significance tests uncounted, as do `validate.py` and `gate.py`. Since the p-value is monotone in `n_tests`, undercounting makes findings look *more* significant — the flattering direction. `generate_series(0, 200)` also truncates the binomial sum.

8. **`missing_required_marts` is enforced nowhere.** `marts.py` computes it, `routes.py` passes it through, `page.tsx` never reads it — every finding panel renders regardless. `page.tsx` also collapses `unavailable` and `empty` into "No powered tests yet", reporting zero where the honest answer is unknown, which is exactly the distinction `marts.py` was built to preserve.

9. **`validate.py` implied vs actual use different denominators.** `implied` averages over all rows in a bucket; `actual` divides by the settled subset only. Settlement arrival is not random with respect to price, so this puts a bias directly into `gap` and into `stderr`.

10. **`devig.market_width` reports `0.0` for a single book.** "No disagreement measurable" renders as "perfect agreement", so the least-evidenced consensus passes the width suppression most easily. `sharp or usable` makes one-book consensus common. Unreadable-resolving-to-zero in a money-path guard.

11. **`clv.py` does not require the entry to precede the close.** With a 1h horizon, a recommendation created inside the final hour is scored against a line observed before it existed. Also, `score_recommendations` only touches unscored rows, so running at 1h then 6h leaves `clv_tenths` a mixture of horizons with no horizon column — and the gate pools them.

12. **`correlation.equicorrelated_joint` silently uses a different rho than asked.** For `n >= 3`, `rho < -1/(n-1)` is not PSD; `_nearest_positive_definite` clips and rescales with only a warning. `implied_correlation`'s residual is therefore *flat* across part of its bracket, and brentq's root there is arbitrary. Same shape as the Shin `z <= _EPS` bug this repo already fixed once.

13. `correlation.py` reachable-range in the `CorrelationUnreachable` message is arithmetically wrong (`joint - residual` should be `joint + residual`); sign error at both ends.

14. **`backtest` uses the wrong standard error for a paired comparison.** `sqrt(0.25/n)` is the null for a single proportion; `gap` is a difference of two accuracies on the *same* games, which needs McNemar's. Too narrow whenever discordance exceeds 25%, which near-pick'em games push past.

15. **`backtest` fits the Platt calibrator on the chronologically last 30%** and evaluates on the earlier 70% — the calibrator has seen later outcomes than the predictions it corrects.

16. `margins.probability_total_over` uses the **margin** SD for totals. Margin and total SD differ in every league (MLB ~3.2 vs ~4.3; NBA ~12 vs ~17), understating total variance 25–40% and making every total look mispriced.

17. `margins.probability_cover` counts **pushes as losses** (strict `>` on integer lines 3 and 7, which carry ~15% and ~9% of NFL mass), and whether it fires depends on float equality.

18. Fee tolerance is absolute (`> 0.005`), so on a 1-contract fill a model 50% wrong passes the check the gate calls stop-the-line. Should be ~1e-9.

## Cross-cutting observation

Three of the four audits independently found the same failure shape, and it is
the one this project already has two lessons about:

> **A guard, its implementation and its test written in one sitting from one
> mental model.** The test inherits the error rather than catching it, and the
> suite goes green over a defect that is invisible in the output.

Instances found this session: the NO-side CLV sign, the orderbook wire format,
per-book sequence detection, `calculate_fee` returning zero, `beats_close`
beside its own contradicting verdict, and two tautological dbt tests.

The countermeasure that works is already written down in `lessons.md`
(2026-08-07): **for any convention with two plausible directions, write at least
one test whose expected value is fixed by definition rather than by reasoning.**
`clv_tenths(close, close, "yes") == 0` and its NO twin discriminate absolutely;
`clv_tenths(480, 520, "no") == -40` did not. The second countermeasure is
capturing the real payload — which is what turned the WebSocket path from
"passing" to "obviously dead" in one command.

## Added 2026-08-07 (evening) — from the first live Odds API call

The key landed and the odds path ran live for the first time. 15 MLB events,
2,096 quotes, 6 credits. **The parser works** — no repeat of the WebSocket
failure. Two findings, one fixed.

### FIXED — `OddsQuote.is_sharp` was a plain method, so every book was sharp

It sat between two `@property` methods as a bare `def`, so `if quote.is_sharp`
binds a method object and is **truthy for all books**. The live capture made it
obvious: all 30 bookmakers reported sharp, including FanDuel and DraftKings.
Now a `@property`, returning exactly `pinnacle`, `betonlineag`, `lowvig`.

Nothing called it yet, so it was a landmine rather than a live bug — it would
have detonated the first time consensus anchoring was wired up, and silently:
"prefer the sharp books" would have meant "prefer all of them", which is the
unweighted average wearing a rigorous name.

### FIXED 2026-08-07 — `h2h_lay` quotes are parsed and stored alongside `h2h`

**Resolution.** Closed at the ingest boundary rather than in the consumer, so
the question of "who builds `quotes_by_book`" never arises: `client._parse` now
classifies every market key explicitly against `PRICEABLE_MARKETS` /
`EXCLUDED_MARKETS`, drops the excluded ones, and **warns** on anything
unclassified. Lay quotes never reach the store, so no downstream grouping can
pool them by accident.

The reason is now measured rather than argued, from the captured fixture
(Mets/Pirates on Betfair and Matchbook): back `2.24 / 1.79` sums to **1.00509**,
lay `2.28 / 1.81` sums to **0.99108**. Devigging removes an overround; a lay
book has an *under*round, so there is nothing to remove and the methods scale
probabilities up instead. Asserted in
`TestLayPricesNeverReachTheConsensus::test_a_lay_book_sums_to_less_than_one`.

Original finding below.



**SEV 1.** We request `h2h,spreads,totals`; the API additionally returns
`h2h_lay` from the betting exchanges (Betfair, Matchbook) — 60 of 2,096 quotes
in this capture. A **lay** price is the other side of the bet: laying a team at
2.22 is a wager *against* them, not for them. Pooling those with back prices
inverts part of the consensus.

`_parse` accepts any market key the API returns and `store_quotes` writes it
with its `market` string.

**Narrowed 2026-08-07:** `consensus_devig` takes
`quotes_by_book: dict[str, Sequence[float]]` — it never sees a market key at
all, because the caller has already scoped it to one market's outcomes. So
`devig.py` is not where this is decided, and reading it will not settle the
question. **The grouping that matters is whoever builds `quotes_by_book`** —
check `engine.py` and `match/linker.py`. If that key is `(event, market, book)`
the lay prices form their own harmless group; if it is `(event, book)` they are
blended into the back prices and part of the consensus is inverted.

Fix regardless of the answer: reject unrequested market keys in `_parse`. An
unrequested market should not reach the store at all, and relying on every
present and future consumer to group correctly is the weaker guarantee.

Note this compounds finding 10 above (single-book `market_width = 0.0`): the
exchanges are also the books most likely to be alone in a region.
